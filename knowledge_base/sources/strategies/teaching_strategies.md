# Teaching Strategies for Learners with Special Educational Needs

## Step-by-Step Instruction

Break tasks into small, manageable sequential steps.
Present one step at a time before moving to the next.
Provide clear visual or verbal cues at each transition.
Celebrate completion of each step before proceeding.

## Scaffolding

Begin with maximum support and gradually reduce assistance.
Initial attempts may require physical, visual, or verbal guidance.
As the learner demonstrates competence, reduce prompts systematically.
The goal is for the learner to complete the task independently over time.

## Prompting

Use a hierarchy from least intrusive to most intrusive:
1. Gestural prompts (pointing)
2. Visual prompts (pictures, diagrams)
3. Verbal prompts (verbal cues or questions)
4. Model prompts (demonstrating the action)
5. Physical prompts (hand-over-hand guidance)

Always use the least intrusive prompt that leads to success.

## Positive Reinforcement

Provide specific, meaningful praise immediately after correct responses.
Use preferred reinforcers identified by the teacher.
Pair social praise with tangible reinforcement initially.
Gradually fade tangible reinforcement as behavior becomes consistent.

## Repetition and Practice

Distributed practice is more effective than massed practice.
Vary the materials and context while keeping the core skill the same.
Review previously learned skills regularly to maintain retention.
Interleave new and familiar skills to maintain engagement.

## Simplification

Reduce linguistic complexity of instructions.
Use concrete rather than abstract language.
Provide examples before asking for responses.
Remove irrelevant information that may cause confusion.

## Gradual Difficulty

Start at a level where the learner can achieve 70–80% success.
Increase difficulty in small increments (10–15%).
Maintain easy items in the activity to build confidence.
Avoid large jumps in difficulty that may cause frustration.
