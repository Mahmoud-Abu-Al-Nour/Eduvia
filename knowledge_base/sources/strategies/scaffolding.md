# Scaffolding in Digital Learning Environments

## Purpose
Provide temporary support structures to help learners perform tasks within their Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), steadily building autonomy and mastery.

## When to Use
Apply during initial skill acquisition, transition across difficulty levels, or when a student encounters unfamiliar content representations.

## Core Principles
1. **Dynamic Support**: Adapt scaffolding level based on real-time performance and error patterns.
2. **Zone of Proximal Development**: Target tasks that the learner can achieve with slight assistance.
3. **Structured Clues**: Provide conceptual, perceptual, and procedural supports.
4. **Planned Removal**: Fade scaffolding gradually as consistency improves.

## Practical Guidance
- Level 1 Support: High visual cues, color-matched borders, reduced distractor count (2 options total).
- Level 2 Support: Standard distractors, subtle visual clues, on-demand hint availability.
- Level 3 Support: Minimal external cues, independent retrieval required.

## Do and Do Not
- **Do**: Maintain identical semantic targets when changing visual scaffolding.
- **Do**: Anchor new concepts to previously mastered schemas.
- **Do Not**: Keep scaffolds permanent, leading to prompt stagnation.
- **Do Not**: Introduce cognitive clutter under the guise of decorative scaffolding.

## Accessibility Considerations
Ensure assistive visual scaffolds (such as color borders) are paired with text or symbol labels to comply with WCAG color-independence criteria.
