# Gradual Difficulty Escalation and Adaptive Progression

## Purpose
Systematically regulate cognitive load and instructional complexity to preserve optimal engagement, motivation, and mastery.

## When to Use
Apply continuously during adaptive engine adjustments, difficulty-level transitions, and recommendation generation.

## Core Principles
1. **Challenge-Skill Balance**: Keep success rates within the 75% to 85% sweet spot.
2. **Deterministic Step-Size**: Increase difficulty only after achieving consistent mastery criteria across attempts.
3. **Graceful De-escalation**: Lower difficulty promptly if consecutive errors indicate cognitive frustration.
4. **Transparent Feedback**: Inform the learner of progress with calm, encouraging visual cues.

## Practical Guidance
- Difficulty Level 1:
  - 2 choices / 2 pairs / 2 items to sequence.
  - Concrete physical objects (apples, balls, cars).
  - Explicit visual cues and full hint availability.
- Difficulty Level 2:
  - 3–4 choices / 3 pairs / 3 items to sequence.
  - Familiar abstract numerals, letters, standard symbols.
  - Mild visual hints on demand.
- Difficulty Level 3:
  - 4 choices / 4 pairs / 4–5 items to sequence.
  - Abstract representations, multi-step comparison or problem solving.
  - Independent retrieval with standard hint availability.

## Do and Do Not
- **Do**: Allow teacher overrides to lock difficulty levels when clinically or pedagogically required.
- **Do**: Provide calm, reassuring feedback during difficulty de-escalation.
- **Do Not**: Jump multiple difficulty levels in a single transition.
- **Do Not**: Base difficulty progression solely on response speed, which penalizes motor-impaired learners.
