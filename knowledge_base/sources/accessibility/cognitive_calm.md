# Cognitive Calm Principles in Interface Design

## Purpose
Minimize sensory distraction, anxiety, and extraneous cognitive load for neurodivergent learners, including students with autism spectrum conditions, ADHD, and sensory processing differences.

## When to Use
Apply across all screen layouts, activity generation prompts, feedback messages, and animation styling.

## Core Principles
1. **Sensory Neutrality**: Avoid high-saturation flashing colors, loud unexpected sounds, or sudden movement.
2. **Predictable Geometry**: Use clear, uncluttered cards, generous whitespace, and stable component positions.
3. **Low-Stress Feedback**: Replace harsh negative buzzers and red crosses with gentle, constructive nudges (e.g., "Good effort! Let's take another look").
4. **Distraction-Free Focus**: Only present elements directly relevant to the current learning objective.

## Practical Guidance
- Backgrounds: Use warm, muted neutral backgrounds (#F8FAFC or warm grays) rather than pure harsh stark white (#FFFFFF) or high-contrast neon.
- Animations: Keep micro-animations under 200ms and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Progress: Show discrete step markers (e.g. 1 of 3) rather than anxiety-inducing countdown timers.

## Do and Do Not
- **Do**: Use encouraging, calm phrasing.
- **Do**: Keep instructions under 15 words whenever possible.
- **Do Not**: Use countdown clocks or ticking sound effects during activities.
- **Do Not**: Include extraneous decorative mascots or animations that compete for attention.
